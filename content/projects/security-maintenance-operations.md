## The Work

Security isn't separated into a different department in my environments. It's woven into the regular maintenance cycle. I track and apply patches, review endpoint protection status on devices and servers, follow up on flagged vulnerabilities, and verify that backups actually work. I also handle access control tasks as they come up, making sure people only have access to what they genuinely need.

This is routine maintenance, and it keeps small problems from becoming organizational incidents.

## Why It Matters

The organizations I've worked with can't afford major security incidents. That means doing the boring stuff consistently: regular patching, antivirus status checks, backup verification, access reviews. Most security problems start small: a missed patch, an account that wasn't disabled after someone left, a backup that hasn't been tested.

Doing this work regularly means those small problems get caught before they become crises. It also means I'm documenting what we've patched, what we've fixed, and what still needs attention, so leadership has an actual picture of the security posture instead of guessing.

## What I've Learned

Small, regular maintenance beats heroic firefighting every time. I've learned that the difference between "we're secure" and "we got breached" is often just one consistently-applied patch or one backup that actually gets verified. And I've learned that security has to be boring to work well. It's the people trying to make it exciting that usually skip the fundamentals.
